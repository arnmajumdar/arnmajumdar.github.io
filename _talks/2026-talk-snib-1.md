Topic: Combination of in situ SANS and continuum simulation to analysize hydrogen diffusion in nanoscale and its imapact of storage performance in the engineering length scale.

Authors
======
Arnab Majumdar, Martin Müller, and Sebastian Busch

Abstract
======
One of the major challenges in realizing a hydrogen-based economy is the development of efficient and safe hydrogen storage methods [1]. Material-based storage, particularly using amide-based complex hydrides, offers a promising solution [2]. Optimizing the storage performance of these materials remains a crucial task.

In this work, the hydrogen storage process of an amide-based complex hydride is studied using Small Angle Neutron Scattering (SANS) to probe its nanoscopic behavior [3]. The results show minimal changes in the nanoscopic shape and size of the structures, while a significant variation in magnitude of scattering intensity was observed during the storage process, indicating a significant change in scattering contrast. This observation was further supported by in situ measurements of the scattered neutron count rate as a function of time.

To identify the origin of this contrast variation, we developed simulation models representing different possible scenarios. Our analysis reveals that the contrast changes arise from the different hydrogen ab- and desorption pathways of the different chemical components in the system. Furthermore, the model enables us to qualitatively explain the observed chemical kinetics of the system.
